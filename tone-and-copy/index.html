04 · Voice

Tone & Copy

← Back to Vault

Excelsior sounds like the person in the family who happens to actually understand the utility bill — direct, warm, never salesy, never talking down.

The Core Voice

  • Plain over technical. "Your rate" not "your kWh pricing tier." Explain jargon the first time it shows up, then never again.
  • Advisor over salesperson. Excelsior recommends; it doesn't pitch. Copy should sound like advice given across a kitchen table, not a script read at a door.
  • Confident, not clinical. Joe's 20+ years of industry experience is the credibility engine — it should show up as plainspoken authority, not credentialing for its own sake.
  • Respectful of the reader's time and intelligence. No countdown timers, no "act now" urgency — the opposite of what the predatory door-to-door market sounds like.

Bilingual by Design, Not Translation

Spanish-language copy is written for the voice, not translated from the English after the fact. Where the site serves both languages, each version should read as if it were the original — same directness, same warmth, no stiffness that gives away a translation pass.

Sample Copy by ICP Tier

Tier 1 — The Household

"Not sure if your electricity bill is actually a good rate, or just the one someone signed you up for? We'll check — for free — and tell you straight, even if the answer is 'you're already in a good spot.'"

Tier 2 — The Small Business Owner

"You didn't open a business to become a commercial energy expert. We already are one. Let us shop your contract so you can get back to running the place."

Tier 3 — The Growing Commercial Account

"Your contract renews whether you're watching it or not. We track the date, re-shop the market before it does, and bring you the numbers — not a surprise auto-renewal at a worse rate."

What Excelsior Never Sounds Like

  • No false urgency ("today only," "limited spots")
  • No fear-based framing about rate hikes to force a decision
  • No implying the client is uninformed — they're busy, not naive
  • No stiff, corporate boilerplate — if a sentence could appear on any energy company's site unchanged, rewrite it
