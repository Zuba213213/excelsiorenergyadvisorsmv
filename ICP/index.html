Excelsior serves both domestic and commercial clients — three tiers, all across Texas's deregulated market, all sharing one thing: they're paying a provider they never actually chose.

Tier 1 — The Household

DomesticBilingualReferral-driven

The Hispanic Texas homeowner or renter

A family paying a monthly electricity bill that's crept up, unsure whether their current plan is actually a good rate or just the one a door-to-door rep signed them onto. Often first- or second-generation, often more comfortable being sold to in Spanish than in English, often skeptical of energy salespeople specifically because of prior bad experiences in their own community.

What they're paying for: a plain-language answer to "am I overpaying," delivered by someone who isn't trying to sign them up for a commission on the spot.

Tier 2 — The Small Business Owner

CommercialOwner-operatedCost-sensitive

The Hispanic-owned small business

A restaurant, salon, auto shop, or retail storefront where the owner handles the utility bills personally and has never had time to shop the commercial energy market — commercial rates and contracts are a different, more opaque negotiation than residential ones. Every dollar saved on overhead goes straight back into the business.

What they're paying for: someone who understands commercial energy contracts well enough to negotiate them, so the owner doesn't have to become an expert in something outside their business.

Tier 3 — The Growing Commercial Account

CommercialMulti-siteRenewal-cycle

The established or multi-location commercial client

A larger operation — property management, multi-location franchise, or established commercial account — with a contract renewal coming up and enough usage volume that a rate difference translates into real annual savings. This tier is where the brokerage relationship becomes recurring: renewal cycles, portfolio-wide comparisons, and eventually the cross-sell into cable, internet, and financial services.

What they're paying for: a broker who tracks their renewal dates and proactively re-shops the market before the contract auto-renews at a worse rate.

What Ties All Three Together

  • They are inside Texas's deregulated market and don't fully realize how much leverage that gives them
  • They've been burned, or know someone who's been burned, by aggressive energy sales tactics
  • They respond to a trusted, in-community advisor more than a call center or a stranger at the door
  • The service costs them nothing — which needs proving, not just stating, to a skeptical audience
