Rule: navy carries authority, steel carries action. Silver is never used for text a client needs to read easily — dividers and labels only.

Typography

Display — Fraunces

Headlines and section titles. A serif with real weight and a slight editorial warmth — steady and established without tipping into the stiff, corporate-generic feel of a typical financial-services sans. Used at medium weight only; never bold, never all-caps.

Body — Inter

All running copy. Clean, highly legible at small sizes, works equally well in English and Spanish copy.

Utility — IBM Plex Mono

Eyebrows, labels, tags, data points. Small, uppercase, letter-spaced. Signals precision — the kind of detail a rate comparison should feel like it has.

The Ascending Mark

Excelsior — Latin for "ever upward" — gets a literal signature: three rising bars used as a section divider in place of decorative numbering. It appears once per page, never repeated as filler, and always in the same three-bar rising form.

On navy backgrounds it renders in silver-to-white; on light backgrounds, silver-to-steel. It should never appear as a full "chart" or "graph" — three bars only, understated, functioning as punctuation.

Usage Rules

  • Backgrounds are always the cool off-white (#F5F7F9) or navy deep — never stark white, never a light-navy "wash"
  • Cards get a thin steel left-border, not a full colored border or drop shadow
  • No gradients, no glassmorphism, no rounded-pill buttons — clean edges, modest 6px radius maximum
  • Every page is single-column, mobile-first — no side-by-side grids at any screen width
  • Bilingual content (where used) follows the same type system; Spanish copy is never italicized or set apart visually — it's the primary voice, not a translation footnote
